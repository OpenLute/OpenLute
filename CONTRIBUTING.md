# Contributing to OpenLute

Thank you for your interest in contributing to OpenLute.

OpenLute is an open-source hardware project, and its success depends on people building, testing, experimenting, documenting, and sharing what they learn.

You do not need to be the original designer to make a meaningful contribution.

## Ways to Contribute

There are many ways to contribute to OpenLute, including:

* Building and testing an OpenLute instrument
* Reporting design or manufacturing problems
* Suggesting improvements
* Contributing CAD or other design work
* Improving documentation
* Testing different materials or manufacturing methods
* Developing accessories or tools
* Sharing build results and lessons learned
* Creating alternative or experimental designs
* Helping other builders troubleshoot problems
* Reviewing and testing proposed changes

Contributions do not have to involve code. **Engineering, fabrication, musical experience, documentation, and practical build experience are all valuable.**

## Before You Start

For significant changes to an official OpenLute design, please start by discussing the proposed change before investing substantial effort in it.

Depending on the nature of the contribution, this may mean:

* Opening a GitHub Issue for a specific problem
* Starting a GitHub Discussion for an idea or design direction
* Discussing the proposed change with the project maintainers

This helps avoid duplicated work and gives the community an opportunity to provide feedback early in the process.

Small corrections and straightforward improvements can generally be submitted directly as a pull request.

## Reporting Problems

If you encounter a problem while building or using an OpenLute design, please report it.

Useful information includes:

* OpenLute design and version
* Component involved
* Material used
* Printer or manufacturing method
* Relevant print settings
* Hardware used
* What happened
* What you expected to happen
* Photographs or measurements, when useful
* Any modifications made to the design

A good problem report helps others reproduce the issue and makes it more likely that a useful solution can be developed.

## Pull Requests

Pull requests are the primary way to propose changes to the official repository.

Before submitting a pull request:

1. Make sure the change is related to the goals of OpenLute.
2. Test the change when practical.
3. Document significant design or engineering changes.
4. Explain the reason for the change.
5. Identify any known limitations or trade-offs.
6. Make sure any new files are organized consistently with the existing project structure.

For physical design changes, include enough information for others to understand what was changed and why.

## Official Designs vs. Community Designs

OpenLute welcomes experimentation and derivative work.

However, not every community modification will become part of an official OpenLute release.

The project may maintain a distinction between:

* **Official OpenLute designs** — designs reviewed and released by the project.
* **Community designs and modifications** — designs created by contributors or other builders.
* **Experimental designs** — ideas or prototypes that have not yet been validated.

A useful modification does not necessarily need to be incorporated into the official design to be a valuable contribution to the community.

## Design Changes

OpenLute is an engineering project. Changes to physical designs should consider more than whether a part can be printed.

When proposing a significant change, consider:

* Structural strength
* String tension and other mechanical loads
* Material properties
* Print orientation
* Manufacturing tolerances
* Assembly
* Serviceability
* Weight
* Playability
* Acoustic performance
* Repeatability
* Compatibility with other OpenLute components

Whenever practical, proposed changes should be tested on a physical build before being considered for an official release.

## CAD Contributions

OpenLute's CAD source is an important part of the project.

When contributing CAD:

* Preserve the project's established organization and naming conventions.
* Avoid unnecessary changes to unrelated geometry.
* Maintain parametric relationships where practical.
* Document significant changes.
* Include relevant version or revision information.
* Do not submit generated files without also providing the appropriate source files when the source is expected for that contribution.

Additional CAD-specific guidance may be added as the project develops.

## Documentation Contributions

Good documentation is a contribution in its own right.

Consider contributing:

* Build instructions
* Assembly instructions
* Print settings
* Material testing
* Hardware information
* Troubleshooting guides
* Measurements
* Photographs
* Build logs
* Lessons learned

If you build an OpenLute, sharing your experience can be one of the most useful contributions you make.

## Respectful Collaboration

Please be respectful of other contributors.

OpenLute brings together people with different levels of experience in musical instruments, CAD, 3D printing, engineering, and music.

Disagreement about a design is expected and can be productive. Personal attacks, harassment, dismissive behavior, or intentionally disruptive contributions are not acceptable.

Please see [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Maintainer Review

Submitting a pull request does not guarantee that a change will be accepted.

Maintainers may request changes, additional testing, documentation, or evidence before merging a contribution.

Some contributions may be valuable but remain outside the official design. In those cases, the project may encourage the contributor to maintain the work as a community or experimental variant.

The goal is to maintain reliable, understandable, and well-tested official designs while still encouraging experimentation.

## Licensing

By contributing to OpenLute, you agree that your contribution will be made available under the license applicable to the files or materials you are contributing to.

Please review [LICENSE](LICENSE) before contributing.

If you are unsure whether you have the right to contribute a particular file, design, photograph, document, or other material, please do not submit it until the rights are clear.

## A Note to Builders

You don't have to be a designer to contribute.

If you build an OpenLute and discover that something works exceptionally well—or doesn't work at all—**that information is valuable to the project**.

Build it. Test it. Document what you learn. Share it with the community.

That's how OpenLute gets better.
